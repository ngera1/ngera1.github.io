MRI Reconstruction with Physics-Informed Conformal Calibration

Nora Gera1, Cassandra Tong Ye1, Kristina Monakhova1
1Cornell University, Department of Computer Science
Poster at i2i Workshop 2026
Poster Arxiv (soon)

Purpose

With the rise of powerful machine learning priors in MRI reconstruction, enhancing performance inadvertently risks introducing hallucinations, or plausible, nonexistent artifacts. Uncertainty quantification methods, like conformal prediction, can help by augmenting the model reconstruction with calibrated upper and lower bounds that provide statistical guarantees on its uncertainty. Recent work applies conformal calibration to accelerated MRI, showing predicted uncertainty maps are correlated with reconstruction errors. However, uncertainty maps are predicted by a downstream module without access to the original frequency-domain measurement, which may result in larger, less physically informed uncertainty intervals. To address this, we propose MRI reconstruction with Physics-Informed Conformal Calibration, MR-PhICC (pronounced morphic), a physics-informed model that parametrizes uncertainty in the reconstruction from k-space measurement.

Background

Machine-learning and other computational methods reconstruct raw frequency magnetic resonance (MR) measurements into images. Introducing these powerful priors risks introducing hallucinations, plausible nonexistent structures. Uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods provide statistical bounds on reconstruction uncertainty.

Deep-learning-only methods, like im2im, do not include a data-fidelity term that ensures the reconstruction is consistent with the measurement, and are thus not physics-based.

Physics-based methods include a data-consistency term and use neural networks as regularizers. However, prior work, like Pixelwise UQ, uses a post-hoc UQ method that is not integrated with the reconstruction.

We ask: Can we apply an integrated approach to uncertainty-quantification for physics-based architectures? That is, can we have UQ informed by intermediates in the reconstruction process rather than only the final image reconstruction?

Method

Primer on quantile loss: Prior work uses quantile loss as a way to generate quantile-conditional reconstructions, but only for a fixed number of quantiles. We instead use quantile loss to provide reconstructions conditioned on any quantile.
